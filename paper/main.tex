\begin{abstract}
Role constitutions are interventions distinct from model capability. A pilot exposed
protocol confounds and ceiling effects. A single-task randomized-block study is
preregistered; results and conclusions remain pending.
\end{abstract}
\section{Introduction}
Can intellectual jurisdiction change visible behaviour and objectively graded performance?
\section{Related Work}
Pending verified literature on role conditioning, instruction controls and repeated evaluation.
\section{Institutional Engineering}
Role defines intellectual jurisdiction; model capability is a separate allocation variable.
\section{Experimental Design}
One fixed task/model, four wrappers, baseline-only calibration, randomized blocks,
external integer grading, separate protocol and substantive outcomes.
Proportions use Wilson intervals\cite{nistwilson}; contrasts use whole-block bootstrap intervals.
\section{Pilot 0: Protocol and Ceiling Effects}
Ninety-six calls over eight tasks. Matched and mismatched strict scores both improved;
protocol reliability and near-ceiling valid outputs prevent role-specific conclusions.
Original failures and graders are preserved without retrospective correction.
\section{Single-Task Role Intervention Study}

\section{Limitations}
Single-task external validity, calibration uncertainty, opaque server sampling/revisions,
time dependence and instruction-length/content differences between wrappers.
\section{Future Work}
